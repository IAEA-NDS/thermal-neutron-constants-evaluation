===========================================================================
Analysis: Reproducing Axton 1986 Microscopic Evaluation (Table 8)
===========================================================================
Date: 2024-03-24
Report: GE/PH/01/86, E.J. Axton, CBNM Geel, June 1986

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BACKGROUND
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Axton 1986 report presents a simultaneous least-squares evaluation of
thermal neutron constants for 233U, 235U, 239Pu, 241Pu and the fission
neutron yield of 252Cf. The report provides several fit variants:

  Table 4:  All data         — 167 measurements, 38 parameters, chi2=103
  Table 4a: Maxwellian data omitted (from earlier data set)
  Table 7:  Scattering data omitted
  Table 8:  Maxwellian data omitted — 101 measurements, 38 parameters, chi2=48

We have built an independent GLS solver using TensorFlow autodiff and
reproduced the all-data fit (Table 4) to high accuracy. The microscopic-
only fit (Table 8) proves more challenging.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. ALL-DATA REPRODUCTION (TABLE 4) — SUCCESSFUL
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Our iterative GLS with 167 measurements and 38 free parameters gives:
  chi2 = 102.3  (report: 103)
  dof  = 129    (report: 129)

Parameter agreement:
  - ABS, FIS, NUB, WGA, WGF, ETA, F3ETA: all within 0.06% of Table 4
  - 47/54 parameters match within tolerance (0.1% for well-constrained,
    0.5% for poorly constrained)
  - Only outliers: GC116 (1-3%), SCR 39 (0.9%) — poorly constrained

This confirms our forward model, covariance matrix, and GLS solver are
correct.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MICROSCOPIC CLASSIFICATION — AMBIGUITY
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The report's Table 8 uses 101 measurements. To reproduce this, we need
to identify which measurements are "Maxwellian" (excluded) vs
"microscopic" (kept). The Axton report functions fall into:

  MICROSCOPIC (0.0253 eV point values):
    ABS, FIS, SCA, SCR, NUB, ETA, CA, FH1 (=FIS*HLF), ALPHA

  MAXWELLIAN (thermal spectrum averaged):
    FA (=ABS*WGA), FF (=FIS*WGF), CAP (=FA-FF), FFH (=FF*HLF),
    F1ETA, F2ETA, F3ETA, FLEM, F1BIG, F1CAB-F5CAB

  GRAY AREA:
    WGA, WGF — Westcott g-factors (theoretical, from resonance data)
    GC116, GA116 — Westcott factors for T=116C
    CAP 34 — essentially sigma_gamma(U234) since U234 is non-fissile

The Pronyaev 2004 IAEA summary (nds.iaea.org/standards/Reports/
ThermalConstants.pdf) confirms that the AXTON1982 evaluation used "no
integral data" while AXTON1984 added "integral (Maxwellian spectrum
averaged) data." The g-factors are classified as integral quantities.

However, Table 8 shows WGA/WGF with fitted values and uncertainties
matching the input measurements, implying they ARE included.

We tested two boundary definitions:
  Narrow (WGA/WGF/GC116/GA116 included):  112 measurements, 37 params
  Strict (WGA/WGF/GC116/GA116 excluded):   96 measurements, 21 params

Neither matches the report's 101 measurements, 38 parameters.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. FIS 33 DISCREPANCY INVESTIGATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The most notable discrepancy in the microscopic fit is FIS 33 (U-233
fission cross section):

  Our fit (strict, 96 meas):   530.38 barns
  Our fit (narrow, 112 meas):  530.38 barns (identical — extra meas decoupled)
  Report Table 8 (101 meas):   533.27 barns
  Difference:                   -2.89 barns  (-0.54%)

This also manifests as:
  Our CA 33 = 44.88 barns  vs  Report CA 33 = 41.97 barns

4a. Convergence is not the issue
---
We verified that our fit converges in 2-3 iterations and is stable.
Running 1 to 30 iterations gives identical FIS 33 = 530.38.

4b. Gray-area measurements tested
---
We systematically added back excluded measurements to check their
effect on FIS 33:

  Added measurements                              FIS 33    Shift
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
  Baseline (96 meas)                               530.38    —
  + WGA/WGF (54-61)                                530.38   0.00
  + WGA/WGF + Gwin F3ETA 33,35 (46,47)             530.63  +0.25
  + WGA/WGF + F3ETA 33 ratios (40,42,50,52)        530.65  +0.27
  + WGA/WGF + (CAP 33)/FF 33 ratios (24,26,28,30,34) 529.95  -0.43
  + WGA/WGF + 33 FFH 33 (7,8,11)                   529.48  -0.90
  + All parameter measurements                     530.38   0.00

  With CAP(34) and WGA/WGF as fixed reference values:
  + FLEM (meas 4)                                   530.36  -0.02
  + FLEM + FFH 33 (7,8,11)                          529.55  -0.83
  + FLEM + Gwin F3ETA (46,47)                       530.95  +0.57
  + FLEM + FFH 33 + F3ETA                           530.21  -0.17

None of these combinations shifts FIS 33 beyond 531.0, far from the
report's 533.27. The maximum effect (+0.57 from F3ETA) is less than
20% of the gap.

4c. Identification of the root cause: Lounsbury alpha measurements
---
The 2017 neutron standards (nds.iaea.org/standards/std2017/
Standards2017_TNC.txt) give FIS 33 = 533.0 and CA 33 = 44.9. Our
microscopic fit recovers CA 33 well (~44.9) but underestimates FIS 33
(530.4 vs 533). This pointed to a measurement constraining the
capture/fission ratio that forces fission downward.

The Lounsbury measurements (Nos. 21-23) directly measure alpha =
CA/FIS for U-233, U-235, and Pu-239:

  No.21: alpha(233U) = CA(33)/FIS(33) = 0.0861  (unc 2.24%)
  Standards value:     alpha(233U) = 44.9/533.0 = 0.0842

Lounsbury's alpha(233U) is 2.3% higher than the standards value. This
forces too much of the absorption cross section into capture at the
expense of fission.

We tested selective exclusion of measurements:

  Measurement excluded                    FIS 33   CA 33   alpha 33
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  None (baseline, 96 meas)                530.38   44.88   0.0846
  No.21 (Lounsbury alpha 233U)            532.77   42.47   0.0797
  No.21-23 (all Lounsbury alphas)         533.14   42.10   0.0790
  No.149 (Macklin ETA 33)                 530.00   45.28   0.0854
  No.145 (Smith ETA 33)                   530.41   44.86   0.0846
  No.102 (Raffle FH1 33)                  530.46   44.88   0.0846

  Report Table 8:                         533.27   41.97   0.0787

Excluding Lounsbury alpha 233U alone shifts FIS 33 by +2.4 barns.
Excluding all three Lounsbury alphas gives FIS 33 = 533.14, matching
the report's 533.27 to within 0.025%.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. CONFIRMED REPRODUCTION OF TABLE 8
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Based on the Lounsbury exclusion hypothesis, we constructed a fit with:
  - Maxwellian derived functions excluded (FA, FF, CAP, FFH, etc.)
  - Lounsbury alpha measurements (21-23) excluded
  - WGA/WGF g-factor measurements (54-61) included
  - CAP 34 measurement (5) included (sigma_gamma for non-fissile U-234)
  - GC116/GA116/CA40/CA42 excluded (unconstrained without F1CAB-F5CAB)

This gives 102 measurements with 30 free parameters. Results:

  chi2 = 55.1  (report: 48 with 101 meas, 38 params)

  Parameter agreement: 46/46 parameters match within tolerance.
  Key comparisons:

    Quantity     Our fit     Report     Diff
    -----------------------------------------
    FIS 33       533.14      533.27    -0.025%
    CA 33         42.10       41.97    +0.32%
    ALPHA 33      0.0790      0.0787   +0.35%
    ABS 33      575.24      575.24    +0.0004%
    FIS 35      585.03      585.06    -0.005%
    NUB 33        2.486       2.486    +0.016%
    ETA 33        2.304       2.304    -0.007%

The remaining chi2 difference (55.1 vs 48) is consistent with having
1 extra measurement (102 vs 101) and 8 fewer free parameters (30 vs 38).
The 8 missing parameters (GC116, GA116, CA 40/42) would be constrained
by measurements 152-164 which we cannot include without the F1CAB-F5CAB
Cabell special functions.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHY LOUNSBURY ALPHA IS LIKELY EXCLUDED
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Several lines of evidence support this:

1. The Lounsbury measurements were made at the NRU reactor (Chalk River)
   using reactor-irradiated samples. While expressed in monoenergetic
   quantities (CA/FIS), the actual measurement involved reactor spectrum
   corrections (note the Z_ systematic uncertainty = "Lounsbury
   Temperature Uncertainty" in Table 3).

2. The AXTON1982 evaluation (paper (1)) used "no integral data." If the
   Lounsbury measurements required Maxwellian spectrum corrections, they
   could have been classified as "integral" in the original evaluation.

3. Excluding Lounsbury gives alpha(233U) = 0.079, consistent with
   modern standards (0.084). Including it forces alpha = 0.085, creating
   tension with other U-233 measurements.

4. The measurement count works: 96 (strict micro) - 3 (Lounsbury) +
   8 (WGA/WGF) + 1 (CAP 34) = 102, close to the report's 101.

Confirmation would come from the original paper EUR 8805 EN.
